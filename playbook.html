one system, two wordmarks

The same six letters, the same star,
and one colour that says which is which.

Everything a customer sees from Oxagen or Stella is drawn from this page: the palette, the type, the two logos, the spinner, the wallpapers, the social art, the ads, and the templates we publish content in.

the decision

Stella’s black and gold wins. Oxagen moves to it.

Two identities existed. Stella shipped black and gold on warm paper — a measured system with a hue clamp on the metal, a contrast ratchet, and a generator that keeps every surface honest. Oxagen shipped a terminal prompt with a vermilion block cursor — a good joke, but a monochrome one, and the cursor idea does not scale past a logo.

So the house adopts Stella’s system whole, and Oxagen gets the wordmark treatment Stella already had right: lowercase name, no mark to the left, one asterisk at the end. The asterisk is where the two brands separate. Stella’s five arms are all the house metal. Oxagen’s five arms are five hues — and one of those five is Stella’s gold, at the exact shipped value.

The parent holds the spectrum

Oxagen is the company; Stella is one of the things it makes. Five hues for the house, one of them for the product. The brand architecture is legible in the mark itself.

Gold is read, not retyped

The gold ray is pulled from Stella’s own token file at build time. Recolour the metal and Oxagen’s star follows. The two marks cannot drift apart.

The cursor is retired

No ›oxagen.sh▮, no block cursor, no glyph before the name. The wordmark is the logo; the star alone is the avatar.

the two marks

One letterform, two stars

Both wordmarks are the string set in JetBrains Mono ExtraBold at 60 units and outlined, so they never depend on a font being installed. The face is monospaced, both names are six letters, and the asterisk is the seventh glyph — which is why the two logos occupy the identical 264×96 box and can be swapped in a layout with nothing else moving.

How it is drawn

The asterisk is never redrawn to make its arms paintable. The real glyph outline becomes a clipping path and five 72° wedges are painted through it, so the coloured star and the gold one are the same curve to the last control point.

Rules

  • ✓Nothing sits to the left of the word. The star is the only mark and it is always last.
  • ✓Minimum width 96 px for the wordmark, 24 px for the star alone. Below that the five arms merge.
  • ✓Clear space is one star width — 32.76 units, or an eighth of the mark’s width — on all four sides.
  • ✓On a photo or a busy field, use the mono cut in warm paper or ink. Never drop a coloured star onto an image.
  • ✓The star keeps its colour on both grounds. The letters swap; the star does not.

Gold never encodes a state. It is identity and at most one primary action per screen. A gold “active” sits 1.3:1 from a warning amber — indistinguishable. Status colour is separate and lives outside this page’s accent.

The spectrum

Five rays, clockwise from twelve o’clock. All four non-gold rays share one OKLCH lightness (L 0.68) at 98% of the chroma their hue can reach there — equal perceived lightness is what makes five hues read as one object rather than five stickers. Every adjacent pair is at least 50° apart in hue, well clear of the 30° floor, so no two arms can be confused at avatar size.

motion

One spinner for both brands

The arms light in turn, clockwise, and the star steps a fifth of a turn each cycle so the lit arm keeps travelling instead of pulsing in place. One period, 1.25s, five steps. It is declarative CSS inside the SVG, so it runs in an <img> with no script, and prefers-reduced-motion lands it on a whole, still star.

surfaces

The star is the only picture we own

Every surface is a ground, one oversized star placed off-centre, and at most two lines of type. A tinted star on a near-black ground goes muddy — every ray lands within a few points of the canvas — so the light goes under the star instead: one soft bloom per ray, at that ray’s own hue and angle, with the glyph laid over at a fifth strength.

Desktop wallpapers

4K, 5K and 6K. The bloom sits right of centre so the icon corner stays clear.

iPhone wallpapers

Three device widths. Aurora for a lock screen, quiet — the star as a hairline — for a home screen full of app icons.

Avatars and app icons

The star alone, at 62% of the tile. Platforms round the corners themselves, so the square cut is the one to upload.

Social headers

Advertising

A kicker in the accent, three short lines set tight in ExtraBold, a rule, the call to action, and the wordmark at the foot. The star bleeds off the top-right corner and never sits behind type.

publishing

How we put content out

Every post, release note and essay ships with a card built from one template: a kind chip in the accent, a title of one to three lines, an optional monospace panel for the thing being shown, the date and desk at the foot, and the wordmark opposite it. The chip is the only place the kind is written — it is not repeated in the title.

  • ✓Show the artefact, not a stock photo. A terminal transcript, a diff, a measured number. The panel exists for exactly this.
  • ✓Titles are lowercase and specific. “parallel tool calls, now measured” — not “Improving Performance”.
  • ✓One claim per card, and the claim carries its evidence. If the number is not on the card, the card is an ad, so use the ad template.
  • ✓Dark for engineering, paper for company writing. Both grounds are ours; pick by voice, then stay consistent across the series.
  • ✗No stock illustration, no gradient mesh, no 3D render. The star is the only picture we own.

the kit

Nothing here is drawn by hand

Every PNG is a render of the SVG beside it, and every SVG is emitted from build/color.py and build/glyphs.py. A colour changes in one place and everything downstream follows on the next run.
